## BIOLOGY (priority #2)

### Topic: Cellular Reproduction / Mitosis
- Terms: mitosis, cytokinesis, chromosome.
- Mitosis stages IN ORDER: 1 Interphase → 2 Prophase → 3 Metaphase → 4 Anaphase → 5 Telophase.
- Interphase = cell doing its job and growing; 3 sub-stages: G1 (growth), S (DNA doubles), G2 (continued growth).
- Mitosis = nucleus divisions: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
- A chromosome is made of tightly wound chromatin.

### Topic: Chromosome anatomy & Cancer
- Centromere: holds 2 chromatids together.
- Gene: segment of DNA that codes for a trait.
- Chromatids: identical copies.
- p53 gene: ensures cells do not divide if their DNA is damaged; detects mutations that could lead to uncontrolled cell growth (cancer), stopping them before they happen. If gene is defective → higher cancer rates.
- Why cancer rates higher in taller/older people: more cells = more chances for a cell mutating; older people's cells divide longer → more odds of mutations.

### Topic: Meiosis
- Purpose of meiosis: to produce gametes (sex cells).
- Mitosis result: 2 identical diploid cells (two copies of each chromosome).
- Meiosis result: 4 different haploid cells (one copy of each chromosome).

### Topic: Mendelian Genetics / Punnett Squares
- Terms: allele, homozygous, heterozygous, genotype, phenotype.
- Key: T=tall, t=short; R=red, r=brown.
- Q1: homozygous tall × homozygous short (TT × tt) → all Tt. Genotype Tt 100%; phenotype tall 100%.
- Q2: heterozygous tall × heterozygous tall (Tt × Tt) → TT, Tt, Tt, tt. Genotypes: TT 25%, Tt 50%, tt 25%. Phenotypes: tall 75%, short 25%.
- Q3: dog red × brown, two red + two brown puppies → parents Rr × rr → Rr 50%, rr 50%; red 50%, brown 50%.
- Pedigree basics: shaded individual = has the trait being followed.
- Pedigree Q: if top parents had 1 more kid, chance recessive = 50%.

### Topic: Molecular Genetics (DNA/RNA → protein)
- Terms: DNA, RNA, mRNA, tRNA, codon, transcription, translation.
- DNA structure: double helix made of repeating nucleotides (A, T, G, C).
- Nucleotide parts: phosphate, base, sugar.
- Transcription: in the NUCLEUS, DNA's info is copied into mRNA.
- Translation: in the CYTOPLASM, the ribosome reads the mRNA and tRNA brings the correct amino acid to form a protein.
- Worked example row: DNA TAC → mRNA AUG → amino acid MET (start). Complementary base pairing: A-T (DNA), A-U (DNA→mRNA), C-G. Codon → amino acid via codon wheel.

### Topic: Evolution & Natural Selection
- Evolution: the gradual change in a species over time, driven by natural selection.
- Natural Selection: nature "picking" the best traits within a population; organisms with best traits survive slightly longer, reproduce, pass beneficial traits on.
- 4 components of natural selection: (1) individuals in a population show variation, (2) variations can be inherited, (3) organisms have more offspring than can survive on available resources, (4) variations that increase reproductive success will have a greater chance of being passed on.
- Adaptations: beneficial traits (created by mutations) that are selected for by natural selection.
- Selection Pressure: a change in an environment that leads to certain traits being selected over others.
- Resilience: the ability of an ecosystem or organism to "bounce back" after a disturbance.
- Population Bottleneck: when a population drops to very low numbers then rebounds; genes of population become similar → less genetic variation.
- Three case studies (selection pressure → adaptation → survival):
  - Hawksbeard: pressure = fragmentation; adaptation = higher proportion of non-feathery seeds; survival = non-feathery seeds fall straight down, higher chance of hitting soil and growing.
  - Juncos: pressure = humans/buildings; adaptation = boldness; survival = bold urban juncos get food and survive.
  - Rats: pressure = poison; adaptation = mutation of DNA in clotting protein making them resistant; survival = resistant rats survive, reproduce, pass resistance on.

### Reading passage: "The Mystery of the Sky-Island Lizards" (apply natural selection)
- Mount Verde (lush) vs Mount Umbra (dark volcanic). Volcanic eruptions + lava "trenches" isolated the populations (geographic isolation).
- Light/Lichen Anoles (Mount Verde): mottled pale-grey skin pattern → camouflage on lichen, invisible to predators.
- Dusk Anoles (Mount Umbra): solid dark chocolate-colored skin → camouflage on dark bark.
- Hybrids: patchy mix → highly visible to predators on both mountains → don't survive.
- Reproductive barrier: lizards born on different mountains develop different dewlap (throat-flap) colors → won't respond to each other's mating display → can't mate → stay two separate species.
- DNA 98% identical → recently diverged from a common ancestor.
- Multiple-choice answers from key:
  1. Original event that isolated the populations → volcanic eruptions that created impassable lava trenches.
  2. Why dusk anoles evolved dark colors → the dark bark on Mount Umbra provided a selective advantage for darker lizards.
  3. Dewlap color difference is an example of → a reproductive barrier.
  4. Why hybrids unlikely to survive → their patchy coloration makes them highly visible to predators in either environment.
  5. "Geographic isolation" defined → a physical divide that prevents two populations from reaching one another to mate.
  6. DNA 98% identical most likely means → the two species recently diverged from a common ancestor.
  7. Statement summarizing natural selection → the environment "selected" for survival those individuals whose traits best matched their specific habitat.

### Topic: Human Body Systems (functions)
- Digestive: breaks down food, absorbs nutrients, removes waste from food.
- Cardiac/Circulatory: moves blood, nutrients, immune cells, hormones around the body.
- Respiratory: gas exchange — brings oxygen in, lets carbon dioxide go.
- Excretory: filters blood, creates urine, stores and removes urine.
- Immune: fights off foreign invaders.
- Nervous: controls all other body systems.
- Muscular: creates movement with muscles.
- Skeletal: provides structure, stores nutrients, generates blood, protects organs.
- Integumentary: body's covering; protects against the outside world, regulates temperature, provides sensory information.
- Lymphatic: a drainage system for cells and tissues.

### Topic: Heart anatomy (4-chambered, labeled)
- Chambers: right atrium, left atrium, right ventricle, left ventricle.
- Valves: tricuspid, pulmonary, mitral, aortic.
- Vessels: superior & inferior vena cava (deoxygenated in), pulmonary artery (to lungs), pulmonary vein (oxygenated from lungs), aorta (oxygenated out to body).
- Flow: body → vena cava → right atrium → tricuspid → right ventricle → pulmonary valve → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → mitral → left ventricle → aortic valve → aorta → body.

### Topic: Digestive system organs (functions)
- Esophagus: moves food from mouth to stomach.
- Stomach: chemically digests food with acid and enzymes.
- Liver: produces bile to digest fat.
- Gall bladder: stores bile.
- Pancreas: produces digestive enzymes.
- Small intestine: chemically breaks down food from enzymes & bile, absorbs nutrients through the mesentery.
- Large intestine: has bacteria living in it, finishes digestion, absorbs water.

## HISTORY — World History Semester 2 (priority #5, lightest)
(Full text captured from the guide. Topics:)

### Industrial Revolution & Economic Theories
- Urbanization: rapid migration from rural/farms into cities for factory work.
- England (Great Britain): pioneering nation that led industrialization (resources, geography, capital).
- Steam Engine: James Watt; most important new power source of the Industrial Revolution.
- Labor Unions: fought for better conditions — ban child labor, worker safety, higher wages. (Did NOT prioritize equal pay for men/women then.)
- Environmental Impact: massive pollution increases → sparked earliest modern conservation efforts.
- Capitalism: means of production privately owned for profit; free markets, competition, laissez-faire (minimal govt).
- Socialism/Communism: community (socialism) or state (communism) owns/shares means of production → classless society. Karl Marx: reaction against inequalities of industrial capitalism.

### Nationalism, Unification & New Imperialism
- Realpolitik: "realistic politics" based on practical needs of the state, not morals/ideology.
- Bismarck (Germany) & Cavour (Italy): both used Realpolitik to unify their nations.
- Germany's Rise: unified 1871 → rapid industrial giant + pioneering social reforms.
- Meiji Reforms: Japan modernized in response to Western pressure, modeling military & legal systems on the West; fast and state-directed (vs the West's gradual industrialization).
- Social Darwinism: pseudoscientific "survival of the fittest" applied to societies; used to justify racism and imperialism.
- Berlin Conference: European powers carve up Africa; borders drawn entirely by Europeans; Africans had no control.
- Independent states: Ethiopia (and Liberia) resisted colonization.
- Sepoy Rebellion: uprising in India after British rifle cartridges greased with animal fat offended Hindu & Muslim beliefs.
- Perspectives: Cecil Rhodes (imperialist, "paint the map red", divine mission) vs Chief Kabongo of the Kikuyu (colonized, felt "strange and helpless").

### World War I & Russian Revolution
- Balkans = "Powder Keg": ethnic tensions fueling nationalism before WWI.
- M-A-I-N causes: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism. (League of Nations came AFTER WWI — not a cause.)
- Spark: assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary.
- Colonial peoples forced/recruited to fight for colonial rulers.
- No Man's Land: barren ground between opposing trench lines.
- Homefront & propaganda: "Don't Waste Bread!" — rationing food to defeat German U-boats (total war).
- Russia's Exit: military losses, food/fuel shortages, opposition to the Czar → Russian Revolution → Russia drops out of WWI.
- Treaty of Versailles: ended WWI; Germans saw terms (reparations, territory loss) as harsh and humiliating.

### Interwar Years & Totalitarianism
- Great Depression: worldwide economic collapse; inflation and unemployment.
- Indian Independence: Gandhi — nonviolent resistance and civil disobedience.
- Mandate System: League of Nations divided Ottoman Middle East among victors → triggered Pan-Arabism.
- Fascism: supremacy of leaders/state, extreme nationalism, brutal suppression of opposition.
- Why dictators rose: Depression chaos + wounded pride → Hitler & Mussolini promised stability + nationalism.
- Japanese Imperialism: expansion for resources/status; brutal invasion of Manchuria.
- Extreme nationalism → state-sponsored atrocities and genocide.

### WWII & The Holocaust
- Appeasement: giving in to Hitler's demands to avoid war; let him seize lands unchecked.
- Lend-Lease Act: US sent military supplies/aid to Allies without entering combat.
- Geographic Disadvantage: Germany's central location → forced to fight a war on several fronts.
- "Final Solution": Hitler's state-sponsored plan to murder every European Jew.
- The Holocaust: the term for this mass genocide.

### Cold War & Independence Movements
- United Nations: post-WWII peacekeeping body to prevent future global conflicts.
- Superpowers: USA and USSR.
- Iron Curtain: Churchill's term for the divide; Soviet-dominated communist governments in Eastern Europe.
- Berlin Wall: built by East Germany to stop citizens fleeing to capitalist West Berlin.
- Marshall Plan: US aid to rebuild Europe; Greece & Turkey aided to prevent Soviet influence.
- Core conflict: US vs USSR struggle for political/economic/military influence (tech, weapons, space race).
- Warsaw Pact: framed as defensive vs NATO; really legitimized Soviet military presence in satellite states.
- Chinese Civil War: communist victory under Mao Zedong.
- Berlin Airlift: first major US-USSR confrontation of the Cold War.
- Korean War: stalemate at 38th parallel; permanent divided Korea.
- Covert regime changes (CIA/KGB): install aligned governments, wipe out rival's influence.
- Domino Theory: justified Vietnam intervention — if one nation falls to communism, neighbors follow.
- Collapse of USSR: Gorbachev's glasnost (openness) + perestroika (restructuring) weakened state control → collapse in 1991.

### Post-Colonial Africa & Modern Conflicts
- Partition of India (1947): Britain split region into India & Pakistan (Hindu–Muslim conflict).
- "Year of Africa" (1960): seventeen African nations gained independence in one year.
- Apartheid: institutional racial segregation & white minority rule in South Africa ("For Use By White Persons" signage).
- Mandela & Gandhi: both used nonviolent protest against racial/imperial injustice.
- Rwandan Genocide (1994): Hutu extremists mass-slaughtered the minority Tutsi.

## FRENCH 3 (priority #3)
Source: "Study guide Final exam French 3 25-26". Exam = verbal questions (conditional) + writing/grammar/reading/listening. Topics listed: the environment, Belgium, future tense, conditional.

### Topic: Le Conditionnel (conditional tense)
- Use: hypothetical/polite requests and SUGGESTIONS (e.g. "Tu devrais faire tes devoirs" = You should do your homework).
- Formation: infinitive (drop final -e for -re verbs) + imperfect endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient.
- Irregular stems (same as future): aller→ir-, avoir→aur-, être→ser-, faire→fer-, pouvoir→pourr-, devoir→devr-, voir→verr-, venir→viendr-, vouloir→voudr-.
- Practice items (verb → conditional form):
  - Tu **inviterais** qui à la soirée? (inviter)
  - Vous **attendriez** où? (attendre)
  - Elle **irait** au parc? (aller)
  - Vous ne **mangeriez** pas à la campagne? (manger)
  - Il **aurait** une grande fête? (avoir)
  - Ils **seraient** heureux de nous voir? (être)
  - Vous **écririez** à votre correspondant américain? (écrire)
  - Elle ne **finirait** pas ses devoirs? (finir)
  - Nous **pourrions** vous aider? (pouvoir)
- Suggestions set (conditional):
  - Tu **aimerais** beaucoup ce film. (aimer)
  - Vous **parleriez** beaucoup avec des amis en français. (parler)
  - Elle **travaillerait** comme professeur d'espagnol. (travailler)
  - Vous **auriez** intérêt à lui répondre. (avoir)
  - Il ne **ferait** pas les devoirs lundi. (faire)
  - Nous **serions** moins stressés dans cette situation. (être)
  - Tu **ferais** mieux de l'appeler. (faire)
  - Comme ça, elle **regarderait** le résultat. (regarder)

### Topic: Le Futur (future tense) — listed exam topic
- Formation: infinitive + endings -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont. Same irregular stems as conditional.
- Difference from conditional: future = "will," conditional = "would."

### Topic: La Belgique (Belgium) — geography
- French-speaking region: Wallonie (Wallonia), in the south.
- Dutch (néerlandais)-speaking region: Flandre (Flanders), in the north.
- Bilingual city (French + Dutch): Bruxelles (Brussels), the capital.
- German is also spoken in a small eastern region.
- Neighbors to label on a map: Luxembourg, l'Allemagne (Germany), la France, les Pays-Bas (Netherlands), la mer du Nord (North Sea).

### Topic: L'environnement (the environment) — vocab & reading/listening
- Edpuzzle theme: "Canicules, sécheresse, incendies — retour sur un été intense" (heat waves, drought, fires — an intense summer).
- Key vocab: la canicule (heat wave), la sécheresse (drought), l'incendie (fire/wildfire), le réchauffement climatique (global warming), la pollution, le recyclage (recycling), protéger l'environnement (protect the environment), le changement climatique (climate change).

### Topic: Writing (production écrite)
- Task type: write an email to a host family (la famille Vosters) about visiting Belgium for 2 weeks — 3 things you would like to do + 3 things you would not like to do + 2 questions. Uses conditional + future. Greeting "Cher/Chère…", closing "Bien à vous,".
